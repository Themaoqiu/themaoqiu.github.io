Grounding-driven Visual Reasoning#

WeVisOne-VL: Unified Visual Perception from Objects to Pixels

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Existing vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable for fine-grained visual perception. In this report, we present WeVisOne-VL (WeChat Vision All-in-One), a powerful VLM that unifies object-level and pixel-level perception through structured next-token prediction. It supports multiple visual tasks including object detection, referring localization, counting, OCR, segmentation, and matting, enabling localization at any user-specific granularity, from points and boxes to masks and alpha mattes. Architecturally, we integrate a lightweight pixel decoder into the VLM via a special &lt;OBJ&gt; token to generate instance-level pixel outputs. We train the model with a three-stage curriculum SFT followed by mixed-task RL. Across over 30 benchmarks, WeVisOne-VL demonstrates leading performance on both object-level and pixel-level tasks, while maintaining robust rejection for object-absent queries. By releasing WeVisOne-VL to the open-source community, we hope to advance VLMs from broad visual understanding toward truly reliable perception systems for real-world deployment.

What Generation Knows: Unlocking Fine-Grained Understanding in Unified Multimodal Models

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Fine-grained visual understanding requires distinguishing subtle differences in local attributes and spatial relations, yet remains challenging for existing vision-language models. Unified multimodal models (UMMs) offer a new opportunity through generation pathways that preserve rich visual information complementary to understanding. However, existing generation-assisted understanding methods mainly rely on generative supervision or explicit generation and have not systematically explored direct use of native generation-side representations. To address this gap, we systematically analyze what generation-side representations offer for fine-grained understanding, finding that they are more sensitive to fine-grained semantic changes, capture task-relevant changes missed by understanding-side representations, and depend on correct spatial organization for this advantage. Guided by these findings, we design GAFU, a post-training framework that unlocks native generation-side information for fine-grained understanding. It compresses generation-side visual representations into compact \(G\) tokens that preserve fine-grained visual details, enabling task-conditioned retrieval by the understanding pathway. Experiments show that GAFU consistently outperforms SFT baselines trained on the same data across diverse visual understanding tasks and UMM architectures, with maximum relative improvements of 20.00\%, 17.94\%, and 8.62\% on VStar-Bench, OmniSpatial, and VSR-Bench, respectively. Overall, GAFU converts complementary information from the generation pathway into gains in fine-grained visual understanding, providing an effective path toward synergy between generation and understanding in UMMs.

Policy-Mask: Budget-Aware Adaptation of Diffusion Features to Discriminative Tasks

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Selecting intermediate features from a frozen diffusion model for discriminative tasks is non-trivial because the useful signal is distributed across the denoising trajectory, network depth, and channels. In practice, this requires choosing a timestep, a block, and a channel subset. Existing methods either fix these choices by hand or optimize only a few extraction hyperparameters, most often the timestep, then reuse the same rule across inputs and budgets. We introduce Policy-Mask, a budget-conditioned policy trained on the downstream objective that jointly selects all three under a prescribed channel budget. Without normalization, the channel-score contribution grows with the number of selected channels and dominates the timestep and block updates. We normalize it by subset cardinality, keeping the three policy heads on comparable scales and stabilizing joint training. At matched budgets, Policy-Mask improves over fixed extraction and feature-filter baselines across segmentation, classification, and semantic correspondence, reaching 38.9 mIoU on ADE20K, 75.4% top-1 on CIFAR-100 at 128 dimensions, and 76.3 PCK@0.1 on SPair-71k. Component-wise ablations show that block selection materially affects downstream accuracy, and the highest recorded channel-budget score occurs at 256 retained channels, identifying a practical operating point for compact diffusion representations. We further adapt Policy-Mask to diffusion transformers, including PixArt-$Σ$ and SD3.5 MMDiT, by replacing U-Net channels with image-token hidden dimensions.

3D LLM#

Deep Search in Real-World Web Environments

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Despite substantial progress in Deep Search agents, their ability to conduct end-to-end research in real-world open Web environments remains insufficiently evaluated. Unlike static retrieval settings, information on the real-world Web is distributed across diverse sources, modalities, and heterogeneous formats, often involving complex interaction mechanisms that require agents to autonomously search for, access, and integrate relevant evidence during execution. To address this gap, we introduce the OpenDS task, designed to evaluate agents' ability to perform multi-step deep search in the live, open Web. This task requires agents to autonomously explore the real Web, discover and connect relevant evidence from multiple independent sources through Web search, browser interaction, and evidence processing, and ultimately determine a unique factual answer. Building on this task formulation, we construct OpenDS-World, a benchmark comprising 200 challenging tasks spanning diverse information modalities, heterogeneous file formats, and interactive access requirements, with independent human verification to ensure evidence validity and answer uniqueness. We evaluate 12 frontier models under a unified tool environment and execution protocol. The best-performing model achieves 39.0% accuracy, while human evaluation across 1,000 independent attempts yields 21.1% accuracy. Further analyses of tool usage, search cost, termination behavior, and failure recovery reveal substantial differences in research strategies across models, as well as persistent challenges in tool coordination and long-horizon search management. OpenDS-World provides a unified evaluation platform for systematically assessing and advancing agents' autonomous deep search capabilities in real-world open Web environments.

IQuest-Q1: Advancing Foundation Capabilities for Agentic CLI Systems

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

We present IQuest-Q1, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) agentic foundation model with 320B total parameters (15B activated). IQuest-Q1 is developed through a three-stage process: (1) stable and efficient pre-training, combining Stable SplitNorm, Muon, and muP with a two-stage curriculum whose second stage increases the coding-data mixture, preserving a wide stability margin between safe and optimal learning rates; (2) fine-grained mid-training, expanding the data mixture across domains and formats and progressively extending the context; and (3) multi-scaffold with multi-version post-training, combining SFT, domain-specialized RL, and MOPD across various scaffolds to integrate complementary training signals and strengthen agentic coding capabilities. Furthermore, model merging combines different experts and checkpoints from different stages while mitigating conflicts among their parameter updates. The resulting IQuest-Q1 demonstrates capabilities in both coding and general tasks. We release IQuest-Q1 as an open-source model to support community research and development.

Toward Recursive Self-Improvement in Physical AI

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) describes a system that improves not only its behavior but also the process by which it improves. Recent digital agents test this idea by rewriting their own code and checking each version with executable tests. Bringing these mechanisms into the physical world requires learning from partial observations through costly trials, while accounting for changing operating conditions and potentially irreversible consequences. We position RSI within robot learning and trace pathways from improving robot capabilities to improving the processes that generate them. We examine how physical experience can strengthen these improvement mechanisms and when the resulting gains can support further self-improvement of the mechanisms themselves. Gains carry forward only when feedback addresses remaining learning needs, the receiving component can use the change, its value survives changes in tasks or hardware, and continued practice remains safe and affordable. Existing studies, concentrated in manipulation, support behavioral improvement, benefits to later learning, and procedure improvement. Some systems also change the improver's knowledge or tools, but the physical studies reviewed here have yet to isolate gains attributable to recursion. We synthesize these findings into evaluation principles and research directions for machines that, trial by trial, safely improve the way they improve.

Multimodal World Model#

Making MLLM Latents Matter: Factor-Aware Predictive Learning for Cinematic Video Generation

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Cinematic video generation requires both temporal inference from limited inputs (i.e., sparse rather than frame-by-frame guidance) and factor-aware control of character, scene, action, and camera. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offer useful priors for both capabilities, but their representations are not automatically actionable for video generation: a Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) may bypass them through native conditioning pathways, and using them does not ensure either factor-aware control or generation-compatible temporal prediction. We propose Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL), which establishes effective MLLM latent use as a shared prerequisite and then targets the two capabilities with complementary learning signals. First, latent-only generator grounding trains the DiT with a frozen MLLM and connector, strengthening the DiT's use of MLLM latents for generation. Second, for factor-aware control, paired videos specify a target-factor change and non-target preservation, and factor-span replacement trains the DiT to realize this relationship in its output. Third, for temporal inference, text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations share a video target, and the trained DiT is then frozen while the MLLM and connector are optimized through it. This final stage trains limited-input representations for generation while retaining complete-observation reconstruction as a complementary training mode. Extensive experiments demonstrate our consistently better performance on three cinematic video generation tasks (i.e., sparse-to-video, factor-aware editing, and multi-shot generation) over baselines and validate FAPL's contributions to MLLM feature use, temporal control, and factor coordination.

Editing Images into Reusable Layers via Compositional Edit-Prior Distillation

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

High-quality image editing requires precise modification of target regions while preserving the content of unrelated regions. Existing editing models produce flattened outputs that limit precise local manipulation and layer reuse, while decomposition methods typically optimize layer recovery separately from instruction-driven editing. To this end, we introduce LayerEditor, a unified framework that couples instruction-guided image editing with controllable generation of reusable RGBA layers. At its core, In-Context RGB-Alpha Token Modeling jointly represents the edited canvas and an ordered sequence of RGB-Alpha layers within a single token sequence, enabling semantic edits and explicit control over individual layer content. To retain the pretrained model's editing capability during adaptation to layered outputs, we propose Compositional Edit-Prior Distillation, which transfers its editing prior to both the edited canvas and the image composed from the predicted layers. To support training and evaluation, we construct LayerEditor-100K, a large-scale paired image-editing dataset with ordered RGBA layer annotations, and introduce dedicated benchmarks for layered editing. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LayerEditor maintains strong editing fidelity while generating accurate RGBA layers, supporting both flexible layer-level manipulation and multi-layer decomposition.

Deep Fusion Memory: A Chunk-Level Parametric Memory for Large Language Models

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

Large language models need effective ways to acquire and use external knowledge beyond what they retain from pretraining. Continued pretraining risks catastrophic forgetting, while retrieval at inference time can add substantial overhead. External parametric memory avoids online retrieval, but constructing its training data requires extensive offline search, and output-level interpolation provides only shallow fusion. We introduce Deep Fusion Memory (DFM), a parametric memory architecture that reduces this construction workload and incorporates learned memory into intermediate hidden states. A causal memory predictor learns to generate memory from the preceding context, using retrieved chunk embeddings from the corpus as supervision. The predicted memory is shared across tokens within each chunk and fused into their hidden states through gated cross-attention. Memory prediction and fusion are trained end to end with the backbone frozen, and inference requires no explicit retrieval. Compared with existing parametric memory approaches, which often construct token-level memory targets, DFM requires $32\times$ fewer retrieval queries and $2048\times$ fewer retrieved items to construct its training data. Across six QA tasks, DFM performs better over the strongest baseline by 12.71% on Mistral-7B-v0.3 and 11.19% on Qwen3-8B-Base. Beyond QA, domain adaptation yields average gains of 6.51 and 4.38 points over Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base, respectively.

LaLa-World: Scaling Latent World Models with Natural Language Actions

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

This paper shows that JEPA-style latent world models (LWMs) can learn across diverse domains and data sources by adopting the language-conditioned interface of video generation models. We establish this approach as an effective and efficient basis for planning with imagination and downstream policy fine-tuning. To this end, we introduce LaLa-World, a Language-conditioned Latent World model that learns from simulation and robot trajectories together with broader image and video data. Two designs make this work. First, we describe both numerical actions and visual changes in language, enabling joint training of a single future predictor across heterogeneous datasets. Second, we build the predictor on a language model, allowing it to interpret language conditions while predicting future latent states. The resulting predictor achieves higher average MPC planning performance than existing LWMs trained separately for each domain or task. Adding image and video data further improves average planning performance and robustness to action phrasing. In a matched comparison with an RAEv2-based video generation model baseline, LaLa-World reaches comparable planning performance with $6\times$ less training cost and $13.9\times$ faster planning. Beyond planning, the pretrained LaLa-World serves as an initialization for LaLa-Policy, which jointly predicts actions and future visual representations. LaLa-Policy performs competitively with Cosmos Policy on LIBERO and RoboCasa, with up to $5.5\times$ faster inference.

Meta Expert: Cross-Layer Expert Sharing for Storage- and Retrieval-Efficient MoE

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

As model parameters scale up, sparsely activated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) approaches have become essential for model scaling. However, mainstream models currently adopt hierarchical architectures, where experts in different layers, although topologically independent, exhibit substantial similarity and redundancy in their stored content. Compared to the hierarchically arranged \textbf{Local Expert} in existing mainstream models, we hypothesize that inter-layer shared \textbf{Meta Expert} hold greater potential. To this end, we conduct a multi-dimensional empirical comparison between Local and Meta Experts, and find that models incorporating Meta Experts exhibit lower parameter redundancy, together with lower cross-entropy loss and higher downstream task accuracy. With the activated quantity and the expert granularity held fixed, \textbf{our model matches the performance of Vanilla MoE using only 70--80\% of the experts}, substantially improving the model's scaling potential. Beyond removing inter-layer redundancy among experts and improving information storage efficiency, our theoretical analysis further shows that, compared with Local Experts, Meta Experts provide hidden states with more flexible residual pathways, thereby improving information retrieval efficiency.

Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models

ArXiv 幻觉翻译

World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.
